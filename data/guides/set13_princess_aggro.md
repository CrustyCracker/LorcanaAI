# Chapter 3: Deck Overview

At the core we are a tempo deck that wants to go wide with a lot of characters. There is one key character, Willow, and a supporting cast making up the core:
{{image-v2:468cc8cc-de6c-4d46-ae26-941b0a33bcfa:100:center}}
Due to princess synergies, Pluto and Aurora are similar cards for the most part. This leaves us with 12 ramp effects, giving us an approximately 97% chance to see ramp on turn two. The ramp effects are what allow us to go wide and play multiple characters a turn to the board. Pocahontas can be considered "pseudo" ramp because of her ability to play another 1c character.

# Chapter 6: Card Choices

{{image-v2:17eb7b50-7d79-49e7-b123-4e166bde2df8:100:center}}
These cards all function very similarly so I've grouped them together.
These cards provide "ramp" for the deck so you can play more characters per turn than you otherwise could.
These cards all reduce the cost of the next character you play.
Pocahontas is here as an honourable mention because she does not reduce the cost of the next character you play but she does let you play another 1c character for free.
They all basically let you play more cards than you otherwise could normally.
This is the core to the deck that allows it to function the way it does.
Definitely play 4x of all of these cards.

{{image-v2:95bf6b48-cc44-4d4b-81f0-91dde669b7c1:40:center}}
The Queen is just a 1 drop.
This deck just needs to play a certain number of 1c characters so that you can mulligan aggressively for key pieces but also not miss on your 1c characters so that you can continue to get wide easier.
It's also important to have a 2 strength character when OTD in the mirror to stop their Pluto from going off and having that explosive turn 2 with 4 characters in play.
It's also important to note that she is a Queen which allows Aurora to get her out for 0.
{{image-v2:2ce9c7fc-b066-4802-a831-0425b712ae43:40:center}}
Thomas is also just another 1c character.
They key here is the 4 willpower.
It's too big so Steel has a fairly difficult time removing him with things such as Look What You've Done or Angel - 624 since they only deal 2 damage.
It's also fairly difficult for Purple decks to remove him because most of the characters are 3 strength.
Thomas also has important synergy with Dale so that you can get free quests in with him being a 4/4 or just being able to trade efficiently with your 1c character.

{{image-v2:89e9a06d-e39a-48de-8b26-72193d38035f:40:center}}
Bobby is just a good card for consistency.
It can help you discard extra uninkable cards you don't need or just help you get closer to the card you need.
Since this deck relies heavily on certain key pieces to function, Bobby is really good at just filtering through your deck to find those cards.

{{image-v2:93883494-3c4a-4ff4-8211-c67e057501c6:40:center}}
Finnick is a good tech option for those song decks.
Being able to just bounce back their singers so they can't sing Under the Sea or other removal is key.
Just buying yourself that 1 extra turn so you can keep questing is important for this deck in those matchups.

{{image-v2:cefb403d-535a-4db9-9438-4cf6a6c8b6cf:40:center}}
Ursula is important so you don't get completely blown out by your opponent if they have a song in their hand.
Taking away cards such as Akood Et Emuti, He Hurled His Thunderbolt, Under the Sea can be game winning because now they can't stop you.
Checking your opponents hand to see what cards are in their hand so that you can play around their next few turns and their removal is also very important for this deck.
This is one of the reasons why this deck can do so well because you can just stop your opponent from doing what they want to be doing.

{{image-v2:7f5061d0-5273-46bf-b439-9b5a4ad113e8:40:center}}
This card is can be treated as Ursula #5 and 6.
Hitting a key card with Mowgli really disrupts their gameplan and buys you time to continue setting up your board.
It can also make inking fairly awkward for your opponent because they're operating with 1 card less.
The main problem though is that you can't choose what your opponent discards from their hand.
So good players will keep an extra non character in hand to play around Mowgli so you will rarely be able to hit the card you actually want to hit unlike Ursula.

{{image-v2:8c86999a-f955-4615-8b4c-8733fa970e2c:40:center}}
David is the most efficient and well statted bodyguard that we have access to.
The 4 willpower as mentioned before is really hard for a lot of decks to deal with.
Being a bodyguard is also important to help you trigger Elinor's ability when you only have 2 other exerted characters in play. David can be the 3rd.
He is also great at protecting your characters since he is a bodyguard which makes it awkward for your opponent to challenge efficiently.
Keeping some of your key characters alive for an extra turn because of David can be the difference between winning or losing the game.

{{image-v2:b7d73838-2776-4ca1-bff3-2f5618e5e5aa:40:center}}
Mike Wazowski is probably the best card that was printed for this deck.
One of the issues with the deck is just not drawing enough cards and you can run out of steam fairly easily.
Mike solves all of this because on play and on quest he draws a card.
So if you can keep Mike alive for a few turns you can really run away with the game because of the amount of cards he will draw you.
Giving your opponent cards doesn't really matter too much when they can't deal with all the characters you're playing.
He is the engine to the deck that makes everything run.
He is also why you play 60 characters in the deck to ensure that you are always drawing a card.

{{image-v2:99faccf7-a15c-4016-99aa-09e2a63f25bd:40:center}}
Nani is not as good as she used to be for the deck before rotation.
Nani used to be a centre piece in helping you set up singer 8 on the board then.
Now she is more used as a card to just draw 1 so that you can find more characters to play and continue building up a wide board.
She doesn't do much more than that but in a deck that wants to keep playing multiple characters a turn she is still the best one at the job.
Not to mention she can also help you find your next Mike so you can continue pressuring your opponent.
She is also well statted being a 3/3 so you can use her to help fight the board when needed.

{{image-v2:309c38c0-f16b-4838-9358-38b7c73dfdf6:40:center}}
With the loss of 2c Tramp this deck really lacked ways to battle OTD.
Most of the characters in the deck have 1 or 2 strength but high willpower.
So Dale acts similarly to 2c Tramp where you can use one of your 1c characters and trade up for a bigger character.
Not only is Dale used defensively, he can also be used offensively.
When you put a Dale down onto the board you can now start questing your characters like Mike, David, Thomas because they all hit for 4 damage now if your opponent wants to trade.
This makes it really awkward a lot of the time because your opponent will have to attack with 3 or 4 cost characters just to take out your 1 or 2 cost characters.

{{image-v2:1e684dda-cf4f-48e8-8819-56aae187ecf8:40:center}}
Elinor is the other key card in the deck that helps you swing the game in your favour.
She just does it all. Lore gain, card draw and damage.
Because your opponent will usually be trading into you, they will often have damage on their characters.
Elinor can easily clean up those characters with her 1 damage.
Card draw from her is also important because we don't want to run out of cards ever so we can continue playing multiple characters per turn.
The lore gain is also really important because we have to quest to get to 20.
So Elinor helps us speed that up. She can also help close the game out like Merlin - Goat used to be for Purple decks to get that last bit of lore to get across the finish line.
She also will run away with the game if unanswered like Mike.
She's just another threat in the deck that your opponent must respect and deal with.

{{image-v2:7783319b-013d-4a9f-9194-b1c9118a59c1:40:center}}
Rapunzel is another new addition to the deck this set.
This deck really lacks ways to remove things or interact with the board and Rapunzel does just that.
She turns all the extra cards you draw with Mike or Elinor into "removal" by bouncing a key card back.
Often times we are stuck and can't quest because there is a Luisa Madrigal or Cheshire Cat on the board.
With Rapunzel we can just simply bounce the threat back to hand so we can keep questing with her.
She can also help bounce singers off of your opponents board so that they can't sing their key cards like Under the Sea.
Overall a great new addition to the deck and solves a problem the deck has.

{{image-v2:88f60808-569d-497b-bb96-a930128a58ec:40:center}}
Amber Champion Mickey is a solid tech option if you are going to be facing a lot of Steel decks.
Giving your Amber characters +2 willpower is key to help you get out of Angel - 624 damage range and He Hurled His Thunderbolt range.
If Steel decks can't efficiently remove your characters they will be in for a hard time against this deck.

{{image-v2:617c1bc9-7f36-4278-94f8-9c2b828f9ae9:40.37356321839081:center}}
Mickey is a great card to tech in if you are playing against a lot of Purple decks because the -2 strength is really annoying for them to deal with.
They won't be able to trade your board very efficiently and you can often just auto win the game because they couldn't get rid of the Mickey immediately.
The issue this card can face sometimes is when you don't have enough characters to shrink your opponents cards so they can still challenge you and take out Mickey.
Rush characters are really annoying because they just remove Mickey immediately when the turn passes to them.
Damage moving cards like Cheshire Cat and Luisa Madrigal are also very annoying because even if they can't deal damage to Mickey they will still be receiving damage in the trade so they can just move damage off themselves to take care of it.

{{image-v2:8824f70d-1cff-4bc1-8aca-25e420ba15a4:40:center}}
Pocahontas is another card that can just win the game for us on the spot.
Now that we have the 2c Pocahontas in the deck we have a good shift target to shift on.
If you can shift Pocahontas against a Purple deck they can no longer challenge and move damage to our characters anymore and we can just quest out to win.
So similarly to Mickey explained above without the downsides of Mickey.
There will be times where your opponent has too many characters on their side of the board and you simply cannot quest out or you will just lose.
So having Pocahontas really helps us get across the finish line vs those decks.


# Chapter 7: Early, Mid & Late Game

This deck plays like a tempo deck with a go wide game plan.
Your game plan will change based on each matchup.
The ideal opening turn would be playing Pluto on turn 1, turn 2 play Willow and exert Pluto to play 2c Pocahontas and another 1c character.
Followed up by a Mike and a David or Elinor.
That being said if your opponent also plays a 1 cost character its usually not worth it to exert your Pluto and just play Willow and another 1c character instead.
In most cases it's not worth it to have them trade Pluto for free because not only have you lost a card but you also still only have 3 characters on the board.

**The Early Game (Turns 1-3)**

- There is one simple goal for the early game: We want to be playing 3, if not 4 characters out by turn 2 and start building up a really wide board.
- So the most ideal hand is something like Pluto, Willow, Pocahontas, 1c character, Elinor or Mike
- We want to be building a big board early so that we can quest in the later turns where your board is too wide your opponent can't deal with all the characters
- When you quest too early your opponent is usually able to deal with your characters which is why we don't want to quest too early
- If we quest too early you *WILL* fall behind probably lose the game because it's very hard to get back into the game

**The Mid Game (Turns 4-7)**

- This is the part where we have a lot of characters out and we want to start chipping away at lore
- We don't want to quest everything where we can potentially lose all our characters
- But we want to quest safe characters and invite your opponent into potential bad trades where we can punish them with a Dale the turn after or an Elinor
- An example of this is when we have a character exerted like Pluto and Thomas or David in play with a Dale in hand
- It is very tempting for your opponent to attack your exerted character and then we can drop Dale and attack over that character with Thomas
- Now Thomas will likely 2 for 1 your opponent taking out 2 characters

**The Late Game (Turn 7+)**

- This is when we really want the game to be over because all the other decks play bigger characters than we do and we won't be able to contest them into the late game
- This is where cards like Elinor are key to help us get the extra lore needed to get to 20


# Chapter 8: Mulligan

THE most important thing in the mulligan is to look for a ramp card (Pluto, Aurora, Willow) and then start altering __**AGGRESSIVELY**__ for the key pieces you need.
The mistake I see most people doing is not altering aggressively enough in this deck and you're just stuck with a very mediocre hand.
Having a __***mediocre***__ hand isn't going to cut it for this deck so you need to be taking advantage of the alter system in Lorcana to open the best hand possible every game.
If I am OTP I will also generally keep Elinor in the blind if I have my ramp characters.
If I am, OTD I wil generally keep 2c Ursula and Dale in the blind if I have my ramp characters.
Otherwise you are really just looking for the ideal opener all the time.
The rest of the game is a freestyle based on what your opponent is doing and you need to adjust based on that.
The mulligan is very different for each matchup so let's dive deeper into it in the following sections.
