# Chapter 3: Deck Overview

**We use the purple cards to draw cards, apply pressure and get high value trades/turns.**
{{image-v2:0c32b048-5eb4-4516-94f4-d33321e8a5af:100:center}}
**We use the steel cards to help control early aggression, have explosive mid game turns, play insanely high statted characters and efficient removal.**
{{image-v2:fbf24ea6-c743-453e-9f6a-7358eed8d378:100:center}}
The point of pairing Purple and Steel together I think is the best colour pairing for the Droid strategy because you gain the most shift targets with the 2 cost Morph to get Syndome out more consistently and you have the best draw cards in the game to keep the deck humming.
This deck is very resource intensive when it comes to shifting and using the droids that you are often starved for cards at low-ish ink counts.
So having the purple cards to help refill after making an explosive Syndrome play I find is essential to the strategy to perform at the highest level.
This is very much a Tempo/Midrange deck with a combo element to it that PS typically never had before.

***Why play this over any other purple deck?***

- You get to break free from the purple game plan where you are playing "fair" and curving up 
- You get some insane high rolls and explosive turns that isn't possible with other decks 
- Also because you get to play steel cards and that is the biggest reason to play this purple deck and not the others
- All the purple decks are running relatively the same generically good cards so it comes down to what the second colour paired with it provides
- Currently I think that steel provides the most with purple. 

***Why play this over any other steel deck?***

- Because we have the most consistent draw engine with the purple cards to help feed this deck because of how resource intensive the Droids are
- The purple cards paired with the steel cards allow you to play very aggressively so you’re always able to constantly apply pressure turn after turn because you don’t run out of cards
- 2 cost Morph allowing Syndrome to have 8 total shift targets in the deck rather than 4 in any other steel deck makes it so much more consistent bringing it out
- Steel covers the weaknesses purple has which is dealing with readied characters.

# Chapter 7: Early, Mid & Late Game

**The Early Game (Turns 1 - 3)**
- There is one simple goal for the early game: Just find your 1c Droid , 2c shift target in Morph or Incrediboy, Syndrome and one of your big Droids. 
- This deck just wants to find its combo pieces and then pop off from there in the mid game. 
- This part of the game we are usually taking on a more control approach to the game because while we want to set up we also have to keep our opponent under control and not let them run away with a huge early lead

**The Mid Game (Turns 4 - 6)**
- This is the part where we start to pop off and take control of the game
- Shifitng Syndrome out on turn 4 and then playing a big Droid for free to start taking over the game
- Not a lot of decks can deal with that so we want to utilize this and take advantage of it to continue pressuring our opponent and snowball the game out of control
- We do this by playing cards like Hades and Demona to draw cards and continue pressuring our opponent with our characters
- The worst thing that could happen is to be stuck at 4-5 ink because we burned all of our resources and can't play our draw cards

**The Late Game (Turns 7+)**
- This is when the game should be getting out of reach because of the advantage we gained in the mid game vs our opponent.
- Now is the time to continue pressuring them and try to close the game out.
- We don't have that much draw so going into a longer grind against other decks that can grind is not ideal because they can eventually choke us out of the game. 
- We are now the beatdown and we want our opponent to be responding to us now



# Chapter 8: Mulligan


(youtube)(LRbUOKmQsiY)(0)
THE most important thing for this deck is actually the mulligan. It's going to set you up for success or make the deck look like a big clunky mess.
Because of how combo oriented the deck is if you mulligan or ink incorrectly you can just lose from there.
So the ideal hand looks something like this: 1c Droid, 2c shift character (Morph/Incrediboy), Syndrome, one of the big Droids

**So following that general guideline, the ideal keeps on the play are:**

- 1 cost character
- 2 cost shift character
- Syndrome
- 6 or 8 cost Droid (if you have all the above)

**The ideal keeps on the draw are:**

- 1 cost character 
- 2 cost shift character
- Syndrome
- 6 or 8 cost Droid (if you have all the above)
- 4c Angel or 4c Droid
- Look What You've Done

On the draw you want to see a lot of defensive cards so your opponent doesn't run away with the game while you're setting up your combo.
One of the best ways to stop Droids from running away with the game is making Syndrome fight and not quest.
So if your opponents' board is too big you will have to fight with Syndrome rather than questing to cheat out the big Droids.

The rest of the game is a freestyle based on what your opponent is doing.

# Chapter 11: Purple Steel (Droids Mirror)

**Difficulty:** 50/50 and play/draw dependant depending on who can get their combo off first or who can disrupt it better.

__**Key cards (Purple Steel)**__
{{image-v2:8557c8fb-04e5-429e-a586-8540df9ee8c5:100:center}}__**Ideal Mulligan**__
**On the play**
- 1 cost character
- 2 cost character
- Syndrome
- 6 or 8 cost Droid
- Look What You've Done or 4c Droid
**On the draw**
- 1 cost character
- 2 cost character
- Syndrome
- 6 or 8 cost Droid
- Look What You've Done or 4c Droid

__**Secret Uninkables**__
There really isn't any as we're just battling for the board with big robots

__**Overall objectives**__
- The mirror is really difficult because your opponent can do what you’re doing but 1 turn sooner when they're on the play, which makes it difficult since they're doing what you do but a turn sooner
- This is why cards like Look What You've Done and the 4c Droid is important because you can deploy them to disrupt your opponents' shift lines so they can't pop off
- If you can disrupt their Syndrome shift and they can't disrupt yours, you are most likely going to win the game because coming back in the Droid mirror is really tough when your opponent has more big bodies out than you do
- There is no real efficient way to get rid of them once they're out on the board except by playing your own
- The other reason why it's so important to stop the shifts early is because if your opponent gets the Droids out into a Demona turn you literally just lose the game and it is impossible to come back from
- So this mirror is a lot about keeping each other off the shifts for the blowout
- This is why I like Incrediboy over Morph because if they are playing 2 damage actions they can snipe the Morph and stop our shift line
- In stalemates cards like Demona and Cheshire cat will help you break the stalemate because you can now efficiently trade into your opponents cards and keep your Droids alive
- There is also variance at play because your opponent can also just see more song removal than you do and just run away with the game
- I would probably ink most of your draw cards first when you are OTD so that you can fight and control the board first
- Once you stabilize that's when you want to use your draw cards to continue closing the gap
- You have to get comfortable with a lot of stand offs where you are both just staring at each other and not doing a whole lot each turn because neither player wants to give Cheshire Cat value and looking for a Demona
